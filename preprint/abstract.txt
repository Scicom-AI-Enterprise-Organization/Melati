Pipelines that turn podcasts into speech-synthesis data, Emilia-Pipe first among them, keep the words and lose what happens between them: laughs and fillers are separated away or never written, emotion is not labelled, and the transcript of a pause is whatever Whisper invents. We present Melati, a pipeline and a dataset of Malaysian podcast speech whose transcripts carry timed tags for non-verbal sounds, emotion spans and the fillers as spoken. Every model in it is a server. On VoxConverse, against the same recordings run through upstream Emilia-Pipe, it keeps 87% of the reference single-speaker speech against 22%, puts a second speaker in 2.2% of its segments against 19.5%, and starts mid-word in 9.2% of them against 37.1%. Its transcriber, released on Hugging Face, is a merge of two whisper-large-v3 fine-tunes that writes text over 5.1% of real voice-free audio against 99.9% for base, with lower error on Malaysian speech (21.6% against 23.4% WER) and on FLEURS. Measuring it as vLLM serves it exposed that Hugging Face's default decoding forbids an empty transcript, which hid both the fix and its cost: without replay of other languages, a fine-tune treats an unfamiliar language as non-speech and returns nothing for 29.6% of FLEURS. Emotion is tagged by seven models averaged, five of them heads we trained, reaching 0.597 unweighted recall on held-out corpora against 0.529 for the released classifiers. Breaths come from a classifier we trained on word-gap sounds, laughs and coughs from a frame-level head that hears them over speech too: on held-out podcasts, the pipeline finds 59.6% of the laughs and 70.1% of the breaths (87% and 83% precise where labelled); Qwen3-Omni finds 46.8% of the laughs and no breaths. Every tag is kept only where its precision was measured. The source code of Melati is at https://github.com/Scicom-AI-Enterprise-Organization/Melati.
